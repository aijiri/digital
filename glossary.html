Glossary

Key terms for Prompt Engineering and Generative AI

← Back to Module 1
Generative AI
A kind of computer program that learns from many examples and creates new things, like text, pictures, music, or video, that look or sound like they were made by a person.
Prompt
The question or instruction you give an AI tool.
Prompt Engineering
The practice of writing and improving prompts so the AI gives you the best possible answer.
CRAFT Formula
A framework for building a strong prompt: Context (background info), Role (who the AI should act as), Action (the specific task), Format (how the output should look), Tone/Target (the style and audience).
Zero-Shot Prompting
Asking the AI to do something with no examples given first.
Few-Shot Prompting
Giving the AI a few examples first so it can learn your style or pattern before it creates something new.
Chain-of-Thought Prompting
Asking the AI to show its thinking step by step, instead of jumping straight to a final answer.
Model-Guided Prompting
Letting the AI ask you questions first, so it can help you build a better prompt together instead of you guessing alone.
Debugging a Prompt
Fixing or improving a prompt when the AI's response doesn't turn out the way you expected, similar to debugging code.
Large Language Model (LLM)
An AI system trained on massive amounts of text so it can understand and generate human-like language. Examples include ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini.
RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)
A method where the AI first searches a knowledge base or documents for relevant information, then uses that information to generate a more accurate, grounded response. In short: search + write.
