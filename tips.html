Tips on Prompting

Simple habits that make your prompts work better

← Back to Module 2

Tip 1: Be Clear

Don't say: "Tell me about dogs."

Say: "Explain dog care, food, and behavior."

Tip 2: Give Context

Add the background information the AI needs before it can help you well.

Example: "Write a children's story about a unicorn in a magic forest."

Tip 3: Be Precise

Say: "List 5 most popular social media apps."

Not: "Tell me about social media."

Roles and Goals

Role is who is talking (teacher, student, expert). Goal is what you want (story, list, essay).

Example: "You are a science teacher. Explain the water cycle."

Positive & Negative Prompts

Positive tells the AI what to do: "Use simple words."

Negative tells the AI what to avoid: "Don't use hard science words."

Use the CRAFT Formula

For a strong prompt, cover all five parts:

  • Context — background info
  • Role — who the AI should act as
  • Action — the specific task
  • Format — how the output should look
  • Tone/Target — the style and audience

Revise, Don't Give Up

Creating a good AI result is a process, not a one-shot task. If the first answer or image is wrong, weird, or too short, don't start over from scratch. Ask yourself:

  • Did I describe what I wanted clearly?
  • Did I include enough details (tone, format, goal)?
  • Did I give too many or confusing instructions?

Then adjust your prompt and try again. Expect to revise two or three times before the result matches what you imagined.
